Behaviour:
Adds film-grain style noise to one plane.
1. The spread of the noise is 1 plus 0.0126984 times (63 minus the q index), so lower quality gives wider noise.
2. A sample distribution is built: for each integer x from -32 up to but not including 32, the normal density at x with mean 0 and that spread is scaled by 256, 0.5 is added and the result truncated to an integer. That many copies of the byte x are appended to the distribution. After the loop the distribution is padded with zeros up to 256 entries if fewer were written.
3. A 2048-entry sample table is filled, each entry being the distribution entry selected by a random number masked to 8 bits.
4. For each of the height rows, a random 8-bit offset selects where in the sample table this row's noise starts. Let the lower bound be the negation of the first distribution entry (that entry is the most negative noise value, so the bound is positive). For each of the width pixels: if the pixel is below the lower bound it is raised to it, if it is above 255 minus the lower bound it is lowered to that, then the row's next sample byte is added. The row pointer advances by the pitch.
Because the sample row starts at a random offset of up to 255, wide rows read up to 255 bytes past the 2048 filled entries (retail behaviour).
